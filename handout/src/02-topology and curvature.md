# Embeddings, Topology and Curvature

A surface $S$ is said to be two dimensional if there exists a injective function from $S \rightarrowtail \mathbb{R}^2$. Meaning we can associate every element in $S$ with a coordinate in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Let us look at the points of a sphere $S \in \mathbb{R}^3$. A sphere is the set of all points in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that are at a distance $r$ from it’s center $M$. Unlike a Ball a sphere does not have any volume, it is if you will, the boundary surface of the ball. Although the sphere “lives” in three dimensions, for a someone living on it the surface itself appears two dimensional.

We can easily proof that the surface of a sphere is two dimensional using polar coordinates. By assigning identifing each point on the sphere with a latitude and longitude angle we can describe the entire sphere with a two dimensional coordinate system.

We say that the spherical surface is embedded in three dimensional space.
